Introduction

Eindhoven grew out of six villages that merged on 1 January 1920: Woensel, Strijp, Gestel, Stratum, Tongelre and the old town. A hundred years later most residents and visitors no longer see those villages, yet the data shows they are still very different places. Six Villages, One City gives each village its own voice, so people can ask a village about itself and hear villages react to each other.

This proof of concept has three villages: Gestel, voiced by the Genneper watermill; Strijp, voiced as footballer Coen Dillen; and Woensel, voiced by the Oude Toren church tower. They are AI characters made by students, not the real places or people, and the page and every answer say so.

How it works

One local language model (gemma3:12b through Ollama) plays every village on a single laptop: no cloud, no API key, no cost, and visitors' questions stay on the machine.

  • A village is a folder with a character (persona.md) and a checked fact list with ids and sources (facts.csv). Adding a village means filling the template, not writing code.
  • Every village has the same CBS 2023 neighbourhood figures (residents, age, income, housing, origin) next to its own history, and compares itself with the Eindhoven average, so differences are real numbers instead of guesses.
  • Live city data: weather and rain (Open-Meteo), fine dust from the city's sensors and Luchtmeetnet air quality, and a city pulse from data.eindhoven.nl.
  • Answers cite fact ids. Code checks flag ids a village does not own and numbers that are in none of its facts. Every answer is logged for a hand check.
  • Villages react to each other: the second village sees only the question and the first answer, never the other's facts, and may speak only with a fact of its own. In Match me to a village mode all three pitch why a visitor would fit their neighbourhood.

How to run it is in the README of the code folder.

Status

The prototype was built and tested in rounds from 2 November 2026, with the client meetings and the Fontys AI and Data Event (17 November 2026) as checkpoints. Each round's problems and fixes are in the test and fix log. Known limits: not all facts are double-checked yet, three of the six villages have no pack yet, and two villages answering takes around 20 seconds on a laptop.

Open questions

Giving a village one voice raises the question who gets to speak for a community and whose perspectives stay unheard. Gestel speaks through a building, Strijp through a real, deceased person. How these choices were made, how visitors read them, and how residents could challenge them are open questions the team is exploring (see the project's GitHub issues).
